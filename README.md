# Project 1: *Manuscript*

Demo/template for our [first projects](https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/project/1/).

> **Students will choose a seminal design text from [readings.design](https://readings.design), read and respond to it, and typeset their selection and reply together.**
>
> The goal of this project is to hone your basic skills in typography, focusing on expression, hierarchy, and form appropriate to a work. You will do this through exploration, trial and error, and responding to critical feedback. And then you will execute this typesetting in code, as a web page

For this website, built around Yoon Soo Lee's essay Judgment and Beauty and my response to it, I wanted to explore the idea of contrast starting with the title, Design vs Judgment. I explored the idea of contrast through typeface, color and size. For the typeface, i explored contrast between serif and sans serif. I set the word "Design" in a serif typeface and in italic because it is more decorative and expressive. While "Judgment" is set in a bold sans serif that feels heavy, direct and blunt, the way judgment states things instead of decorating them. For color, I was inspired by the debate between designers by their printed dialogue by Jan van Toorn and Jean Leering, where each voice is set in its own color, red or blue, being the most contrasted colors and the text shifts across the page with large margins every time the speaker changes. I used the same strategy: blue appears when the text talks about design and beauty, red when it talks about judgment, and a large margin shift separates the section titles from the body text. Size adds another layer of contrast, as short statements like "Fuck beauty." break out of the calm body text at a much larger scale, like a thought interrupting the reader. Finally, the section headings are sticky, staying with you as you scroll through each section, to emphasize the idea that judgment always stays with you throughout the design process, and in this case, throughout the website.
